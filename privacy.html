Parcel Permit Scout: privacy policy

Effective 17 November 2026; updated 21 November 2026 to cover Microsoft Edge, 26 November 2026 (version 1.4.8) for the Owner contacts page, 27 November 2026 (version 1.4.9) for its Business owners part, and 27 November 2026 (version 1.5.0): CityView portals are read one page at a time. Applies to the Parcel Permit Scout extension for Chrome and Microsoft Edge, version 1.0.0 and later.

In short: everything the extension works with stays in your own browser on your own computer. Nothing is sent to the developer. The only websites it contacts are the public permit portals you choose, from your own browser tab, to read their pages, and, only when you press the button for it, BatchData (with your own key) to look up contacts for your lots, and Colorado's open business register or OpenCorporates (with your own key) to look up the businesses that own your lots.

What the extension handles

  • The spreadsheet you import: parcel numbers, addresses, your notes and any other columns in it.
  • A file of owner names, if you import one.
  • Owner contacts (version 1.4.8), if you add them: phone numbers, emails and Do Not Call, TCPA or litigator flags for the owners of your lots, from a results file you add, a BatchData lookup you run, or what you type in; and, if you save them, your BatchData API key and the address of your own lookup website.
  • Business findings (version 1.4.9), if you look them up or add them: for lots owned by an LLC, a corporation, a trust or another owner that is not a person, what a public business register says about it (the business as registered, its status, its registered agent, its principal and mailing addresses, where that came from and how well its name agrees with the owner on the tax record); and, if you save one, your OpenCorporates API key.
  • What it reads on the permit portals you choose: the permit and inspection records those sites show to the public, or to you if you are signed in there.
  • Its own settings, activity log and run reports, and the places (portal addresses) you set up.

It does not collect your name, email, browsing history, location, passwords, payment information or anything about websites other than the portals you choose.

Where it is kept

In your browser's storage for this extension, on your computer only. There is no account, no server, no analytics, no advertising and no tracking. The extension contains no code loaded from the internet.

What leaves your computer

  • Visits to the permit portals you set up. When you start a check, the extension reads those sites in your own browser tab, the same way you would by searching and clicking yourself. It only searches and reads; a guard refuses anything that would submit, apply, pay, schedule or change something. On a website it does not search by itself (OpenGov towns such as Vail, and, from version 1.5.0, CityView portals such as the City of Thornton's), it reads only the page you have open there, when you press "Read this page". If you are signed in to a portal, it uses your existing session in that tab. It never reads, asks for or stores a password.
  • A BatchData lookup, only if you run one. On the Owner contacts page, if you save your own BatchData API key and press "Look up … with BatchData", the street address, city, state and ZIP of each of those lots are sent to BatchData (api.batchdata.com) with your key, so BatchData can return the contacts it has. BatchData's own terms and privacy policy apply to what it receives, and it charges your account. Your key is kept in this browser only and is never put in a backup file or any file the extension makes. The browser asks your permission for BatchData's address before the first lookup; removing the key removes that permission.
  • Your own lookup website, only when you open it. If you set up a lookup website on the Owner contacts page, "Open lookup" opens it in a normal tab with the lot's details you put in its address (for example the owner's name and the street address), as if you had typed them there. The extension never reads that tab.
  • A business lookup, only if you run one. On the Owner contacts page, "Look up … Colorado businesses" sends the business name of each of those lots (an LLC's or a corporation's name, never a person's name, address or parcel number) to the Colorado Secretary of State's open data site (data.colorado.gov), which answers with what its public register holds. "Look up … with OpenCorporates" sends the business names and the state, with your own OpenCorporates key, to OpenCorporates (api.opencorporates.com). Trusts are sent only if you tick "Also look up trusts". Those sites' own terms and privacy policies apply to what they receive. The browser asks your permission for each site's address before its first lookup; your OpenCorporates key is kept in this browser only and is never put in a backup file or any file the extension makes.
  • A state's official business search, only when you open it. "Open … search" opens that state's own search page in a normal tab and copies the business name for you to paste. You search there yourself, and the extension never reads that tab.
  • Nothing else. No information is sent to the developer or to any other third party.

Access to each portal is asked for one at a time, with the browser's own permission prompt. You can remove it at any time.

Files on your computer

Workbooks, CSV files, reports and backups are saved to your Downloads folder when you press a button for them. By default a backup of your readings is also saved there after every run; you can turn that off in Settings. The optional evidence recording (Settings) is time-limited, stays on your computer, and leaves out anything typed into the page.

How your information is used

Only to do what the extension is for: checking the building permits and inspections of the lots in your spreadsheet, showing you the results, and keeping the owner contacts and business findings you add. It is never sold, shared, used for advertising, or used to decide creditworthiness or lending. The use of information received complies with the Chrome Web Store User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements, and with the Microsoft Edge Add-ons developer policies.

Deleting it

In the extension: Clear all results and Remove workbook and results delete what is saved (a backup is offered first); Remove all contacts, Remove all business findings and Remove key on the Owner contacts page delete the contacts, the business findings and the BatchData or OpenCorporates key. Removing the extension from your browser deletes everything it saved. Files you downloaded stay in your Downloads folder until you delete them.

Children

The extension is a work tool and is not directed at children.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version is published here with a new date, and the extension's notes for that version say what changed. A change that would send any information off your computer would be asked of you in the extension before it happened.

Contact

Questions or problems: open an issue on GitHub, or use the contact email shown on the extension's Chrome Web Store or Microsoft Edge Add-ons page.
